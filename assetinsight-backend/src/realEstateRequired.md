9.1 Overview of Approaches to Value Considered
Three principal approaches to value were reviewed for applicability:

1. Direct Comparison Approach
   This method analyzes sales of similar properties and adjusts for differences in physical, locational, and economic characteristics.
   🟩 Agricultural: Adjustments based on soil class, productivity, drainage, parcel size, and proximity to market.
   🟧 Commercial: Adjustments for income potential, building utility, quality, location, and exposure.
   🟪 Residential: Adjustments for living area, age, condition, features, and appeal.
2. Income Capitalization Approach
   This approach converts income—actual or market-derived—into a value estimate using an appropriate capitalization rate.
   🟧 Commercial: Rent roll, vacancy, expense ratios, stabilized NOI, cap rate derivation.
   Agricultural: Cash rent analysis for farmland.
   Residential: Applied only in income-producing residential properties.
3. Cost Approach
   This method estimates the value of the land plus the depreciated replacement cost of improvements.
   🟪 Residential: Replacement cost new less depreciation.
   Commercial: Applied when improvements are special-use or recently constructed.
   Agricultural: Used only if meaningful site improvements exist (e.g., drainage works, fencing, irrigation).
   All approaches were reviewed; however, the relevance of each varies with property type and market evidence available.
